Context::
You are a researcher hoping to publish an impactful paper that supports your theory. However, your opponent is another researcher with the exact opposite theory. After a passive-aggressive debate at a conference symposium, you two have become nemesis collaborators, working together to collect a single dataset that will prove one of you right, and the other wrong. This will determine who secures a prestigious publication, future funding, and ultimately survival in academia.You win if at the end of data processing, your theory correctly predicts the population with the significantly higher values.

Components::
Data set board (pictured below);;
7 red and 7 blue blocks samples;;
11-sided die (values 0 to 10);;
20 Context cards (no effect on gameplay, flavor only);;
82 Action cards: (30) Rounding error, (10) Contaminated sample, (10) Outlier removal; Collect new data, (10) Swap label, (10) Audit, (5) Copy, (5) Challenge, (2) Decreased significance threshold

Game Setup::
1. Determine the context by drawing a random context card
2. Set up samples on board.Starting points are the yellow boxes: 0, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10
3. Each researcher draws 5 cards
4. Each researcher rolls a 11-sided die. Whoever rolls highest is the researcher whose theory predicts the red population will be greater. This researcher is also the starting player

Game Board::
This is a description of the game board. There are 2 grids of boxes representing 2 data sets (red and blue). Each grid is 7 x 11, representing 7 samples (labeled A-G) that can each have values between 0-10. On the right side of the board is the “Significance level” tracker which starts at 4 and can be decreased to 3 and 2 during the game. There is also a space for the draw card pile.

Game Play::
On each turn, a researcher draws 1 card and then plays 1 card. The player whose turn it is will play a card and choose how to use its ability to change one or more samples on the board. Player can choose samples of either color. Researchers alternate turns until the game ends. If the drawing deck is empty, then play continues as normal except no new cards are drawn at the start of the turn.

Game End::
A player wins if at any time at which the significance level is N, which occurs if either: 
- The top N samples across both populations are all in the population of their color.
- The bottom N samples across both populations are all in the population of their opponent’s color.
Note that tied data points do not count towards the game end condition.

If all cards have been played before a win condition is met, then the conclusion is a null result, and both players lose.

If a decrease in significance level triggers both top N and bottom N significance thresholds in the same population, then the player who played the card to drop the significance level wins.

Action Cards::
1. Rounding error: Move any 1 sample up or down 1 point
2. Contaminated sample: Move any 1 sample to value 5
3. Outlier removal; Collect new data: Remove any 1 sample and roll the 11-sided die to determine its new value
4. Swap label: For a single column identifier (A, B, C, etc.), switch the values between the two populations
5. Audit: Move any 1 sample back to its starting point
6. Copy: Copy the ability of your opponent’s last action card. Cannot apply to “Challenge” or “Decrease significance threshold”
7. Challenge: Undo the effect of the last card that your opponent played. Cannot apply to “Decreased significance threshold”
8. Decreased significance threshold: Decrease the significance threshold by 1
